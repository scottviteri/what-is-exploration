# Proposed adaptive-weight study

23 September 2026. Prepared research protocol; **not launched, preregistered, or a new computation allocation**. No outcome must favor a native objective. Existing depth-four results are design evidence now. The current campaign and its protocol remain frozen.

The study has two questions. Can retained decoder cuts make a changing finite native objective cheaper to certify? Separately, can a declared weight-selection rule improve a fixed independent capability audit? A positive answer to the first is useful even if information or Brier wins the second. The full eventual positive-weight theorem motivates the construction but is not the finite experiment's empirical endpoint.

## Objects, access, and quantities

Every arm receives the same finite class of candidate controlled models, action/observation interface, and collector family of all randomized full-history policies. It knows this class, not the actual world. Information and posterior Brier use the same declared uniform planning prior. A decoder is stochastic, world-independent and specific to its target. Record all actions alongside observations; deterministic target trees may use an equivalent observation-only encoding only when its known tree reconstructs every action.

Collection time `t` counts interactions, target depth `n` counts steps of a counterfactual intervention, and optimizer wall time is a third resource. No learned-policy training time is involved in this first study. The primary independent endpoint is worst native deficiency at depth four, `A_(t,4)`, after training on a declared depth-three emphasis library. A complete audit evaluates all 32,768 deterministic depth-four trees. An incomplete search is a lower witness, with a separately justified upper bound, and cannot silently become a complete audit. Depth-three training error is also reported, especially for minimax, but winning its identical optimized metric is not evidence of transfer.

A rich mathematical objective uses `w=(1-epsilon)v+epsilon b`, where `b` is the explicitly fixed rational native-table background in THEORY.md. The finite optimizer retains the actual coefficients on selected encodings; it does not renormalize them. Every endpoint reports its selected loss interval, omitted mass, full finite loss interval and final-weight finite regret bound. It reports no eventual regret bound absent an independently supplied collection-time lag. Weighting syntactic encodings is conventional and sensitivity to that convention is a design variable.

## Track A: correctness and computation on identical objectives

Before comparing objective quality, validate the new bridge against the frozen monolithic and decomposition implementations on exactly the same models, collector horizons, target kernels, costs and policy constraints. Use the frozen 16-case grid, including the small case where decomposition was slower, rather than choosing its successes. Initially use saved model arrays and finite deterministic libraries; then add a few literal rational randomized targets whose full action/observation laws are independently reconstructed.

The new bridge uses the frozen target dual cuts but preserves them across weight changes. Numerical paths are currently unrun. Verify the following before treating any new result as evidence:

1. Reconstruct every raw controlled mass, flow equation, target law and replayed experiment independently. Include null incumbent histories, reordered targets, changed kernels, duplicate encodings, zero coefficients and very small positive coefficients. Ensure random target actions are retained.
2. Compare cold monolithic, cold decomposition and retained-cut decomposition under the same sequence of nonnegative weights. Recompute each final master dual bound and each feasible-policy upper bound. Verify certificate identity, objective costs, coefficient-rounding correction and every cached cut on original probabilities. Export the actual duals and master arrays; a witness hash alone is not validation. Persist the returned `witness_store` with the cache. The prepared bridge refuses a reused cut without a matching original dual and reconstructs it under the current frozen geometry.
3. Confirm that fresh bounds agree within the prespecified tolerance. The prepared bridge pads target intervals by the frozen `2e-7` witness tolerance and defaults to a `1e-6` selected optimization gap. This differs openly from the archived prototype's `2e-7` aggregate gap. Use `1e-6` for **all** compared new backends, and retain raw residuals and gaps. Any tighter comparison must first validate tighter interval arithmetic; never relax the declared tolerance after a failure.
4. Check the rational target compiler against enumeration at tiny depth, and validate the conversion between frozen deterministic trees and one canonical full-history rational encoding. The exact compiler exists; the frozen-tree conversion and arbitrary model bridge do not yet have independent tests.
5. Validate reward-face constraints separately before using them in the new bridge. They are presently rejected. The old pinned planner can provide favorable baseline controls if its identity and feasible region are independently checked; its runtime is charged accordingly.

**Objective equivalence and optimizer selection are separate checks.** The [saved optimum-selection analysis](../target_selection_2026-09-23/interim_reports/2026-09-23_optimum_selection/README.md) finds alternative saved policies with the same native training optimum to numerical accuracy but different depth-four errors, for weighted loss and minimax. Consequently, a backend equivalence check requires identical objective/feasible-region definitions, independently valid bounds and objective agreement at the declared tolerance; it does **not** require identical policies or held-out errors. A held-out difference alone is not evidence that the formulations disagree. Conversely, matching held-out errors does not validate either formulation. Report the returned-policy differences as selection effects, and do not replace frozen representatives after inspecting their audits.

For each future warm/cold comparison, retain the final objective identity **separately** from the run's selection provenance: solver/version/options, initialization, initial cut-cache hash, weight sequence, random seed, tie-breaking rule, and returned-policy hash. Compare a cold solve and every prespecified warm-start path at the same final coefficients, recording both final objective intervals and the independent audit. This distinguishes a change of objective from a change of selected approximate optimum. The present code's objective and policy identities already differ, but the complete run-provenance exporter and prospective secondary-selection constraints still need implementation and validation.

Prespecify three serial timing repeats, rotating method order. Count build, target construction, master solve, replay, decoder work, cut insertion, weight updates, certificate verification and any imported-cache creation. Report both cold total cost and incremental reweight cost, with the old cache cost amortized over the explicitly stated number of objectives. Report export and independent scientific audit separately and also give end-to-end time. Do not count an old 40-second timeout as a successful 40-second runtime. Record memory per process and state when high-water memory includes previous work.

A backend success means equivalent certified finite objectives at lower charged cost on the prespecified large cases, with small-case overhead and failures disclosed. Faster approximate answers without matching bounds do not establish this. A failure or no consistent speedup remains a useful outcome; the archived one-repeat speedup is motivation, not the new result.

## Track B: objective quality under charged compute

Use one independently validated backend for all native arms. Keep the model, collector horizon and reporting budget fixed within each comparison. The primary floor is `epsilon=1/20`; study `1/100` and `1/10` only as prespecified design-set sensitivity, unless a later confirmation protocol explicitly includes all three. A small floor reduces the worst-case tail bound while weakening individual capability calibration. It is not selected after observing baseline losses.

Use a common initial library of 16 encodings, a common finite training pool, and common expansion checkpoints at 16, 32, 64 and 128 deterministic depth-three trees. Map each deterministic tree to one canonical rational-table encoding by assigning a fixed action on unreachable full histories. Preserve its chosen background mass and any separately evaluated rational targets. Add a small, fixed randomized-target subset to test the rich-target interface. Freeze that subset and its identifiers before quality runs; changing the encoding convention is a separate sensitivity analysis. A finite background truncation can have a sizable interval even when the finite optimizer closes accurately.

| Arm | Prespecified rule | Intended claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed weighting | Uniform emphasis over the same selected library, plus the common positive background | Reference finite approximation; not an intrinsically preferred weighting |
| Tractability adaptation | Emphasis proportional to reference weight divided by `1+c_j/s`; `c_j` is cumulative charged target-decoder time and `s` is the median positive initial per-target cost, fixed after the charged initial sweep | Potential reduction in objective/certification cost; no coverage theorem |
| Hard-target adaptation | Positive initial emphasis; multiplicative updates from certified interval midpoints, with a declared rate and interval-error accounting | Conditional finite robust guarantee for averaged realization plans; last iterate reported separately |
| Direct minimax | Minimize the maximum deficiency on the same training pool | Strong control for the identical training audit; not automatic depth-four superiority |

For the tractability rule, failures consume their elapsed budget; never omit them from cost or use held-out errors to choose weights. If no positive initialization cost is recorded, use a declared timer-resolution floor. In implementation `s` is supplied explicitly. A simple fixed-weight solve using the final cost-selected weights must be included in the timing analysis to distinguish better weights from mere cache reuse. A cheap-looking objective may have poor independent capability; retain that result.

For the hard-target arm, start uniformly. The proposed exact-arithmetic theory uses `eta=min(1,sqrt(log K/R))` for an announced number of rounds. Implement the finite schedule for a fixed `K` block, and restart its guarantee when the library changes; do not apply the fixed-library theorem across unaccounted expansions. Stop only at the common charged wall budget, preserving incomplete rounds. Report certified best-response gaps and error intervals. For the full robust-objective guarantee, each best-response error includes the selected numerical gap **plus omitted background mass** (or a tighter justified tail upper bound); a fixed nonzero tail leaves a nonvanishing error floor. The implemented floating update has no rigorous update-rounding budget yet; numerical MW behavior is exploratory until this is supplied. Average realization weights, not conditional policy rows. The fixed final-weight score and the independent audit are evaluated on the returned average; keep the last iterate as a secondary result without granting it the averaging theorem.

The full robust-background epigraph would be a useful additional control, but it is not implemented in the bridge. Implement and validate it before claiming the hard-target procedure approximates its optimum. Direct finite minimax without the background is a distinct benchmark, labeled accordingly. Do not relabel a sparse set of dual weights as the rich objective, and do not assume every minimizer of an optimal dual weighting is minimax: the two-bit counterexample in THEORY.md rules that out.

Include information gain, posterior Brier gain, uniform actions and the historical 16-target native anchors. Information and Brier already have finite-history dynamic programs; do not force them through a slower artificial solver. Report their actual planning/certification time and no fictitious need to consume the entire native budget. Exact Bayesian next-raw-observation losses may remain additional prespecified descriptive baselines if inherited unchanged; they differ from posterior Brier gain. Removed ICM/RND/feature objectives are not restored, and empirical observation-label entropy is not state-occupancy entropy.

For both information and Brier, obtain the policy with the smallest **training** worst-target audit within 0%, 1% and 5% of the attainable intrinsic-return range from optimum; report actual recovered return regret and the nominal-zero slack of at most `1e-10`. These are favorable controls, not worst-over-optima envelopes. Use the independent depth-four audit only after that selection. Where feasible on the small design cases, also inspect the worst audit over the exact/near-optimal face, and mark its absence elsewhere. Do not turn a default-representative gap into an all-optima claim.

Native methods receive the same scrutiny for selection among optima. Preserve ordinary solver representatives, and prospectively add a weighted-loss control that minimizes the **training-depth** maximum inside a certified final-weight sublevel set. For minimax, a separate proposed lexicographic control minimizes the training-depth weighted mean inside its certified minimax sublevel set. These define additional methods; they do not guarantee better depth-four performance, and no current outcome is replaced. Selection informed by the observed depth-four results is exploratory and needs untouched confirmation. Report spread among available representatives without calling it the complete optimal-face range.

If the first-stage minimization optimum has certified bracket `[L,U]`, a secondary constraint `F(pi)<=U+tau` guarantees original-objective regret at most `U-L+tau`, provided the recovered policy is independently checked to satisfy that constraint. The first-stage gap is part of the budget even when the nominal extra slack `tau` is zero. For a selected rich-background truncation, the full finite objective adds its omitted-tail bound; exact optimality or full eventual optimality does not follow. The new bridge currently rejects these constraints, so neither this control nor its validation is claimed implemented. All secondary optimization, validation and selection time must be charged.

The supplied [13:43 figure assessment](../target_selection_2026-09-23/interim_reports/2026-09-23_figure_assessment/ASSESSMENT.md) clarifies the existing-data boundary: the frozen queue has no depth-four audits for the 1%/5% posterior controls. Such audits would be a separately recorded evaluation extension, using the already selected policies. The four saved K=128 policies with failed held-out decoding should likewise be recovered by auditing those original policies, not reoptimizing. This existing-evidence recovery belongs to the campaign owner; the new confirmation classes in this protocol remain a separate proposed study.

## Model split, budgets, and falsifiability

All previously read cases and depth-four outcomes belong to the design set, including the September-15 failed screen and the current campaign's incomplete snapshot. Use them for debugging, cost pilots and negative-case diagnosis only. The illustrative dry-run proposes twelve **new** random classes: four/eight candidate worlds, three hidden states, concentration `.2/1/5`, and model seeds `926101/926102`. These seeds are fixed in DRY_RUN.json but not yet checked against every archive; perform a metadata-only nonoverlap check before freezing the confirmation queue. If they overlap, replace all colliding seeds by the next unused values before generating outcomes, recording the change. No unseen outcome has been inspected in this pass.

Proposed collector horizons are `t=3,4`, training depth is three, and independent target depth is four. Proposed native computation checkpoints are 2, 10 and 40 seconds; selection seeds are `926201/926202/926203`. Treat seeds as repeated computation on a model class, not independent environments. A continued anytime trajectory may supply all three checkpoints, but charge all prior selection/solver effort and compare baselines at the same acquisition horizon. A solver iteration that exceeds a checkpoint does not retrospectively create a certified policy at that earlier time. The prepared bridge records evidence availability and rejects late master/oracle evidence, but the frozen solver has a soft time limit; a later execution harness must enforce a process wall cap and account for all validation/selection overhead.

These dimensions are a reviewable candidate grid, **not** a resource commitment. A later owner must first measure audit cost and determine a bounded allocation. By timings alone, choose feasible horizon/budget tiers and freeze the exact common queue before generating confirmation outcomes. Reductions must apply across methods and be reported. Do not quietly launch the theoretical grid or full depth-four sweeps under this assignment. A 40-second planner cap says nothing about the much larger total audit cost.

Primary analyses use paired common-case differences in `A_(t,4)` and time-to-final-weight certification, summarized separately. Publish full curves and per-case intervals, not just a winner count. Suggested numerical tie threshold is `1e-6` only when certified intervals justify it. The method comparison is descriptive for this finite test suite; model seeds do not justify a population claim about all environments. At every budget report attempted, complete, failed, incomplete and unaudited cases, plus the paired denominator. Missing cases remain visible instead of being scored as losses or removed from the registered count.

Useful outcomes are explicit:

- Lower certification cost with unchanged or worse independent capability supports the computational track only.
- Lower independent worst-target error on the frozen new classes supports that finite transfer claim; it does not establish eventual sufficiency, universal dominance or training efficiency.
- A training-depth gain accompanied by a depth-four loss reproduces the known transfer risk and falsifies a broad coverage interpretation.
- Failure against favorable information/Brier controls rejects the claim that the selected native approximation is better on that suite. Keep it as a result.
- A large omitted-mass interval or unclosed solver gap identifies approximation limits, even if the observed point estimate looks favorable.

# Reward compatibility follow-up

This implements the diagnostic already registered in the parent MANIFEST and PROTOCOL. The primary campaign and its frozen sources remain unchanged. Eligibility uses the four prespecified pilot classes, budgets 3, 4, 5, and information/Brier collectors whose independently checked lower error to a fixed reference exceeds 0.02 + 1e-9. Sort by case, budget, method, reference; retain all eligible jobs up to the registered cap of 60. JOBS.json records eligible, included, excluded and ineligible comparisons with their immutable primary evidence. No selection by diagnostic outcome is possible at preparation.

For original reward R and fixed target record F, compute the largest R(pi) subject to delta(K_pi,t,F) <= 0.02. Subtract from the unconstrained optimum of R at the same budget. A positive numerical lower bound on this regret means recovering this particular capability requires reward sacrifice. A near-zero upper bound exhibits compatibility; it does not imply every optimum, a typical learning algorithm, or the originally selected policy has that capability. Diagnostic policies use the reference during selection and never replace primary collectors. Each reference is tested separately: the resulting policies need not simultaneously reproduce all references. Information/Brier remain full-world posterior potentials under the original uniform prior, not raw-observation prediction rewards.

## Formulation and numerical bounds

History-action realization weights x satisfy root and branching flow constraints. For leaf history h, its world-independent action weight is w_h(x); its raw model likelihood is p_q(h). A randomized decoder is represented jointly by z_hy = w_h(x) G_hy, with z >= 0 and sum_y z_hy = w_h(x). Thus the simulated target law is sum_h p_q(h) z_hy, linear in z. Absolute-value auxiliary variables impose worst-world total variation at most epsilon. Posterior leaf-reward coefficients are fixed because w_h cancels from the posterior over q; the expectation is linear in x. The LP therefore jointly selects the collector and a decoder, globally over full-history randomized policies.

Solve with epsilon tightened by 1e-7, then physically replay the conditional policy and a normalized nonnegative decoder. Require its actual total-variation upper bound <= 0.02 - 1e-9. Report the true requested cap 0.02; the tightening is numerical protection, not a new scientific tolerance. Relax the final LP constraint in its box-repaired dual certificate by 1e-7 times that constraint's nonpositive dual multiplier to obtain an upper bound on the best reward at the requested cap. The replayed feasible policy gives a lower bound. The independent unconstrained Bellman recurrence supplies R*. Report the resulting regret interval, without treating float64 as interval or exact arithmetic. Use 2e-7 only as the numerical comparison threshold; do not label small positive costs mathematically zero.

Every finite target here can be reproduced exactly by executing its original three-step policy and extending arbitrarily, so the constraint is feasible at every tested budget. A solver failure or timeout is computationally unresolved, not evidence of incompatible capability. The checker independently reconstructs physical dynamics, all LP constraints and reward coefficients, replays primal/dual bounds, and verifies the full-source decoder. It invokes no optimizer. It tests reward compatibility, not a minimum-deficiency value for the new policy; its decoder only needs a feasible upper bound.

## Execution and controls

No diagnostic numerical workers begin until the primary supervisor reports complete. One worker on CPU 10, one thread, 8 GiB address-space cap, 180 seconds per job including independent checking (LP limit 150 seconds), and the original campaign's absolute eight-hour deadline apply. The original 4 GiB output allowance and 15 GiB free-disk reserve cover both stages. Either parent or local STOP file prevents further work and terminates only this supervisor's own active child group. No solver retries, overwritten failures, increased tolerances, or new model classes are automatic.

Before freezing, test known positive information/Brier sacrifice in a one-step two-bit model, exact zero sacrifice in a symmetric model, singleton-class vacuity, and rejection of changed decoder, reward cost, and target binding. These are small implementation checks, not campaign findings. VALIDATION.json binds their outputs. Source and input snapshots are verified per job. Failed and unrun jobs remain visible in STATUS.json. This package makes no manuscript, formal ledger, Git or Overleaf changes.

Independent audit of the adversarial native-learning pilot
17 November 2026

Scope

This audit checks the finite experiment semantics, numerical evaluation,
training/evaluation separation, reproducibility, and saved evidence. It does not
prove convergence of the learner, approximation to the eventual J_w score,
sample efficiency, or generalization beyond the declared worlds and budgets.
All LP bounds are floating-point numerical evidence, not exact rational or Lean
certificates. Their residuals, dual corrections, and rounding allowances remain
visible in the saved oracle outputs.

Independent mechanics checks: PASSED

Command:
  python3 test_pilot.py --output independent-checks.json

The test source uses an independently written observation-law evaluator rather
than the pilot's table builder. It checks 87 source/target channel constructions
and probability normalization. Fifteen decoder-LP comparisons confirm that
decoding the deterministic target's observation string is equivalent to decoding
its reconstructed full action-observation history. All 27 target laws are also
checked directly against full-history execution.

Analytic examples verify the exact irreversible error curve: a collector reading
left with probability s has errors (1-s)/2 and s/2 on the two bit targets. The
fair mixture therefore has errors 1/4 and 1/4. In the compatible environment,
reading both bits exactly simulates all 27 targets. The pure-noise target has
zero deficiency in all three environments, including the noisy-reading case.

The three-bit counterexample gives profiles (0,1/2,1/2) for reading the first
bit and (0,0,1/2) for reading the first two. The second record strictly improves
the first while their largest native errors agree. This supports the explainer's
distinction between the finite minimax preference and the weighted reference
score's strictness property; the numerical checks do not constitute a new Lean
formalization of that example.

Numerical oracle checks

The pilot's sparse decoder LP is compared with the older independently authored
dense deficiency LP in Experiments/src/idexp/lp.py on 18 source/target pairs.
Every unrestricted and per-action-floor reference collector is replayed against
all 27 targets with that independent evaluator. Compatible and irreversible
unrestricted optima agree with their analytic values 0 and 1/4. The learned
parameterization imposes a .005 lower bound on each action probability, so the
separate floor-constrained reference is necessary when attributing residual loss
to training. Twenty-five deliberately nonoptimal dual choices check that the
box-corrected weak-duality formula remains a lower bound on an elementary LP
with known optimum 2.1.

Training and selection checks

The learner receives empirical response tables only. Its policy has seven
history-indexed action rows, and its decoder inputs have 36 full collected
histories; neither has a world-indexed input. World labels enter the training
loss because the protocol explicitly grants labelled resets to every world.
Fail-fast replacements of the true-table and exact-oracle functions do not
interrupt learner runs. Input tables remain unchanged.

Repeated seeded runs are identical apart from timing fields. A callback that
mutates its supplied policy, decoder and archive and returns a replacement does
not alter training. Each callback receives a deep copy and its return is ignored.
The supplied runner evaluates true and empirical models only after run_training
returns, providing an additional separation from selection and learning.

Adversarial, random, and hardest_uniform methods have identical initial
collectors, paired first candidate batches, and matching non-generator update
and candidate-query budgets. The learned generator's extra updates are counted.
Random selection is reconstructed from its separate RNG stream and does not use
fitted errors. Both hardest-target methods select the largest fitted candidate
error; hardest_uniform leaves its generator uniform and performs no generator
updates. Selection-time candidate decoders are retained so that their errors can
be replayed before the accepted decoder is refit.

Development replay: PASSED

Command:
  python3 test_pilot.py --results development/initial --allow-legacy-candidates \
    --output development-replay.json

All nine development runs, 36 checkpoints, and six reference policies passed.
The audit independently recomputed 1,674 decoder-LP values and reproduced all
sampled datasets from the frozen sampling source. This older development run
predates saved candidate-decoder witnesses. Eighteen candidate-score replay
checks are explicitly skipped; the recorded selection scores and RNG choices
remain checked. The legacy exemption is not permitted for the main cohort.

Main cohort replay: PASSED

Command:
  python3 test_pilot.py --results main --output main-independent-replay.json

All 72 registered runs and 432 saved checkpoints passed. The checker verified
every frozen source hash, regenerated all 18 sampled datasets, and replayed all
1,080 selection-time candidate scores from saved decoder witnesses. There were
no skipped candidate-witness checks. The independent dense LP computed 15,228
decoder values across 564 distinct source profiles, covering every saved true
and empirical target profile. Six saved reference collectors also passed
independent replay. Archived decoder errors, fitted empirical losses, paired
budgets, target acceptance, and score-independent random choices agreed with
the saved records.

The largest distance of a dense-LP value outside the corresponding sparse-LP
numerical interval was 1.98e-9, below the comparison tolerance of 2e-8. This
small discrepancy is retained in main-independent-replay.json; the intervals
are numerical estimates and do not have an exact enclosure guarantee. The full
suite, including the mechanics and learner checks above, completed in 61.3
seconds with no failures. The replay record retains hashes of the independent
test source, the older dense-LP source, and each saved training and audit file.

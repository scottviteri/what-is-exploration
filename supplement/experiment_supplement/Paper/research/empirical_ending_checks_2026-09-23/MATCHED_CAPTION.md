# Caption for the matched-reward alternative

Use the world, policy, record, decoder and audit definitions in [CAPTION.md](CAPTION.md). Both figures retain every original class. `CANDIDATE_22_MATCHED.pdf` places the reward-matched comparison in the main image; `REWARD_MATCHED_DIAGNOSTIC.pdf` shows it more closely.

**Finite capability and posterior reward can favor different collectors.** (a) The six ordinary method families are compared on the hardest four-interaction target after collecting three action–observation pairs. Information gain and Brier concern the entire world posterior; the categorical pseudo-count baseline preserves its declared density-model specialization. Weighted native minimizes a uniform mean over 128 depth-three targets, while finite minimax minimizes their maximum. Bars enclose the original saved representatives and numerical intervals.

(b) For each class, fix one weighted-native reference by lexically selecting its saved cell ID, without using the new held-out audit. Select a second policy by minimizing the same depth-three maximum subject to retaining at least that reference's Brier reward, with the literal floor `R_B(control) >= R_B(reference) - 1e-10`. This is a **native minimax planner with a Brier constraint**. It is not ordinary Brier optimization or a generic near-optimal Brier learner. Positive differences favor the weighted reference. The reference itself is feasible, so the control's training maximum cannot be worse at an exact optimum; its performance on longer targets is a separate measured question. Every depth-four witness is retained and checked for both policies.

(c) Actual Brier sacrifice is the fraction of the attainable reward range forgone. The matched control is permitted at most the reference's sacrifice and can retain more reward than the constraint requires. The weighted reference and matched control are the same policies used in (b); unconstrained minimax retains its original representative envelope. The constant-reward class has no defined normalized fraction. This does not assert equal planning computation.

The comparison is post-design, uses the original heterogeneous 22-class grid, and does not test independent generalization, all optimizers, eventual `J_w`, or universal experiment dominance. The corresponding directed record-deficiency check is reported separately: better Brier reward and a smaller worst-target scalar do not by themselves establish a simulation of the other complete experiment. Favorable and unfavorable cases are retained. The preceding five-percent comparison and the old failed sixteen-target screen remain available; the new diagnostic does not rewrite those results.

The complete result is 6 weighted-native wins, 1 numerical tie, and 15 losses, using the prespecified `1e-6` classification tolerance. The median audit difference is `−0.0030462851`. Twenty-one of these selected record pairs are numerically incomparable in the two directed deficiency checks; one is equivalent within tolerance. This is descriptive evidence about this finite mean and constrained finite maximum on these existing classes.

To fix the objects and direction: a world is one candidate environment model in the supplied finite class. A policy chooses actions using its acquired history, without knowing which world is actual. After three action–observation pairs, its record experiment is the family of record distributions indexed by world, denoted \(K_{\pi,3}\). A target is a specified native intervention and its resulting record distribution. The directed deficiency \(\delta(E,F)\) asks how accurately a randomized decoder applied to an \(E\)-record can simulate an \(F\)-record, minimizing over decoders and taking the worst error over worlds. The decoder can depend on the target but must be the same in every world. Smaller deficiency means a better substitute in that direction; it is not the probability of identifying the actual world.

The weighted-native planner minimizes the uniform mean of these deficiencies over the 128 deterministic depth-three targets. The native-minimax planner minimizes their maximum. Write these training losses as \(L_{128}(\pi)\) and \(M_3(\pi)\), respectively. Both collect three interactions. Evaluation retains that same three-interaction collector and measures its worst deficiency to all 32,768 deterministic depth-four targets. The fourth interaction belongs to the hypothetical target, not an extra collection step. The models remain the same. Consequently this is a test of transfer to longer interventions, not a test on unseen world classes or independent training/test datasets.

For each class, the matched diagnostic fixes one saved weighted-native policy \(\pi_N\) by lexical cell ID, without selecting it using the new evaluation scores. Its comparator is a native-minimax planner with the literal constraint

\[
\min_\rho M_3(\rho)
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
R_B(\rho)\ge R_B(\pi_N)-10^{-10}.
\]

Here \(R_B\) is world-posterior Brier improvement under the declared uniform prior: expected squared posterior norm minus squared prior norm. It is a reward for information about the world label, not categorical next-observation prediction loss. The control may earn more Brier reward than the reference; “matched” means a floor, not exact equality. The reference itself is feasible, so an exact optimum cannot have worse *training* maximum. No such argument guarantees improvement on the depth-four evaluation. The completed numerical solutions meet the recorded reward floor and optimization-gap tolerance; they are not Lean proofs of optimizer selection.

**Substantive reassessment: what question does the experiment answer?** Added 24 September 2026, 00:53 UTC, following the lead author's scope correction. This is a change to the interpretation and proposed role, not a caption qualification or a change to the frozen data.

There are two distinct scientific questions. An **order question** asks whether one experiment can replace another through a world-independent decoder, and whether an objective respects such comparisons. A **scalar tradeoff question** asks which experiment has the smallest chosen aggregate of target errors. The order leaves incomparable experiments unordered. Choosing a worst-target maximum, a prior, or positive weights can resolve those comparisons, but each supplies an additional preference. None becomes the order merely by being called native or prior-free.

The current evaluation is the second kind:

\[
 A_{3,4}(\pi)=\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal T_4}
 \delta(K_{\pi,3},K_{\sigma,4}).
\]

Its two horizons retain their previous meanings: three acquired interactions, four interactions in each alternative target. The maximum expresses a preference for limiting the largest remaining simulation error. It is a legitimate operational robustness criterion when that is the question of interest. But ranking two policies with positive errors adds that preference; a smaller maximum need not preserve every capability of the other record. The all-depth, eventual zero-error criterion characterizes complete exploration. It does not uniquely justify using a positive fixed-depth maximum to rank incomplete collectors.

This distinction also concerns strictness. If \(E\) simulates \(F\), composing decoders shows \(\delta(E,T)\le\delta(F,T)\) for every target \(T\). Taking a maximum preserves that weak inequality. It need not preserve a strict improvement: the worst target may remain unchanged while another capability improves. The repository already has a literal native example, not just an arbitrary pair of vectors. A world consists of two bits \(U,V\); root actions reveal exact \(U\), noisy \(U\) with error \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), or exact \(V\), and later observations are fixed. Exact \(U\) strictly dominates noisy \(U\), yet both worst-native-target errors are \(1/2\), because neither reveals \(V\). This holds at every positive collection and target horizon. See [the scalar-choice exposition](../../draft/intrinsic_objective_choices.tex) and `scalarBit_native_audit`, `scalarBit_native_audit_upTo`, and `scalarBit_strict_finitary` in [ScalarBitCausal.lean](../../../Formal/Formal/ScalarBitCausal.lean), registered under `claim:scalar-objective-native-obstruction`. Thus maximizing the negative audit has no general strict-monotonicity guarantee. This does not prove that a particular unrestricted minimax optimum in this dataset is dominated; it identifies why the maximum cannot serve as a neutral test of strict order-respect.

**The favorable baseline theorem applies here.** Fix a finite nonempty world class, a prior \(\alpha\) positive on every world, and finite record signals. Write \(E\succ_B F\) when \(E\) simulates \(F\) but the reverse is impossible. Whole-world information gain and posterior Brier improvement satisfy

\[
 E\succ_B F\ \Longrightarrow\
 I_\alpha(E)>I_\alpha(F),\qquad R_B(E)>R_B(F).
\]

One can see exactly why. Couple the experiments through a garbling: draw the world under \(\alpha\), draw \(X\) from \(E\), then draw \(Y\) from the world-independent decoder producing \(F\). Let \(p_X,p_Y\) be the resulting world posteriors. The lost scores are

\[
 I_\alpha(E)-I_\alpha(F)=\mathbb E\,\mathrm{KL}(p_X\Vert p_Y),
 \qquad
 R_B(E)-R_B(F)=\mathbb E\|p_X-p_Y\|_2^2.
\]

Both are nonnegative and vanish only when the two posteriors agree on every positive-probability coupled record. In that case the reverse kernel \(R(x\mid y)=\Pr_\alpha(X=x\mid Y=y)\), with arbitrary stochastic rows at null signals, simulates \(E\) from \(F\) in every world. Full prior support is what makes the conclusion hold for every world, including rare ones. Thus a strict irreversible garbling must lose score. The usual log/quadratic potentials and their divergences are classical; see [Gneiting and Raftery, Section 3](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf). The simulation conclusion uses the equality argument just given, not strict propriety alone.

Existing checked routes include the two identities in [PosteriorLossIdentities.lean](../../../Formal/Formal/PosteriorLossIdentities.lean), the equality/reverse-simulator and strictness declarations in [PosteriorGarblingStrict.lean](../../../Formal/Formal/PosteriorGarblingStrict.lean), and the causal feasible-family statements `information_maximizer_blackwell_admissible` and `posteriorPotential_maximizer_blackwell_admissible` in [CausalInformationAdmissibility.lean](../../../Formal/Formal/CausalInformationAdmissibility.lean). The latter generic potential is on posterior densities and retains its stated convex-domain assumptions; for the finite-world Brier specialization, use the positive prior to convert densities to posterior probabilities. I inspected these routes and the corresponding support entries; this review adds no formalization or new build claim.

Consequently, at the same fixed collection horizon, an exact maximizer of either score cannot be strictly Blackwell-dominated by another feasible policy. This does not assert that it dominates every competitor or minimizes \(A_{3,4}\). Nor does it certify a saved approximate solution as globally admissible. Scores here concern the entire world posterior and terminal gain at the chosen horizon (equivalently the undiscounted telescoping increments). The conclusion does not automatically extend to truncated world features, different predicted variables, discounting, or unrestricted later process comparisons. In particular, the separate delayed-test counterexample remains consistent: superiority of eventual delayed evidence is different from dominance by the scoring deadline. Complete finite-world posterior objectives also have the paper's positive process theorem; this dataset does not separate them from eventual `J_w` on that property.

The eighty-four incomparable pairs out of eighty-eight original comparisons, and twenty-one out of twenty-two matched comparisons, should therefore be treated primarily as evidence of **different compromises**. The remaining pairs are equivalent within tolerance; neither collection reports a numerical strict-dominance pair at its classification threshold. These findings do not diagnose violations of order-respect. They also do not prove all selected policies lie on the full feasible frontier: pairwise incomparability among sampled representatives cannot exclude a third policy dominating one of them. The exact archived-table Lean example strengthens the incomparability evidence for its one pair, not an objective-level violation.

The six/one/fifteen matched result remains informative, but its conclusion is precisely about scalar transfer: a maximum over depth-three target errors, constrained by the reference's Brier reward, often yields a smaller maximum over depth-four target errors than the selected finite mean does. Matching the posterior reward removes one unequal-reward explanation. It does not make the maximum an objective-independent standard or turn those fifteen scalar improvements into fifteen dominance improvements. Keeping the collector fixed while lengthening the target avoids testing the identical numerical optimization problem, but retains the same maximum-based evaluation preference. No fresh-world generalization or universal capability advantage follows. Likewise, strict monotonicity of rich eventual `J_w` does not prescribe how its weights rank incomparable processes, and the 128-target finite mean does not inherit every rich-objective guarantee merely from its name.

An order experiment would instead make the two simulation directions the primary evidence. For an exact dominance claim, it would exhibit a valid forward garbling and prove reverse deficiency positive; a small numerical forward upper bound supplies only approximate simulation, not exact zero. For a claim about a deficient exact optimizer, it would additionally establish that optimizer's reward optimality in the same feasible family. For a time-limited selected policy, it would report selection/optimization error separately. For an eventual process claim, it would need the appropriate all-continuations or growing-record argument, not only three-step records and four-step targets. Finite full-support whole-world IG/Brier are positive controls for fixed-horizon strictness, not candidates from which such an exact dominated optimum should be expected.

The existing package already supplies useful order diagnostics without more optimization. Its two-direction certificates can illustrate incomparable evidence and, where interpreted, the opposed decision uses behind it. The existing exact/noisy-bit construction supplies a checked strict-order control and shows why max aggregation may tie a real improvement. Reporting target profiles would expose more of the compromises than one maximum, but the finite deterministic-target profile alone must not be advertised as a complete representation of Blackwell order either.

My revised recommendation is to retain this package as completed supporting evidence about finite scalar preferences, longer-target transfer, and computational verification while the lead author decides the empirical ending's scientific question. It could support an explicit discussion of what an objective adds beyond the partial order. It currently cannot supply a headline that native minimax better respects that order than IG/Brier, or that these finite results demonstrate the distinctive generality of eventual `J_w`. The paper's process counterexamples and positive theorems carry those different claims under their actual assumptions. No choice between the two candidate main figures is made or approved here; resolving the question comes before choosing the figure. All frozen results, favorable baseline cases, and adverse native results remain intact.
